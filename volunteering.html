My story

I grew up in Lagos, Nigeria as a Northern girl. The phrase I heard often was: “If you were schooled in the North, you would be married by now.” I thought it was an exaggeration until I moved to Niger State for my undergraduate degree at FUTMinna and met girls who had genuinely been taught by their communities that education was not their destination.

For a long time I carried two sides of myself: a researcher working on intelligent systems, and a female STEM advocate fighting for girls who looked like me. These two felt like separate lives, until I realised they were always the same story. I became an engineer because someone made me believe I could. Everything I have done since has been an attempt to be that person for someone else.

Behind every number below is a young person who now sees themselves differently: as a builder, a problem-solver and a future engineer.

An investment in me is not an investment in one person. It is an investment in every girl in Northern Nigeria who cannot be in certain rooms but benefits from the fact that I was.

Enactus FUTMinna

Project Manager  ·  Schneider Electric × Enactus Nigeria Outreach

As Project Manager of Enactus FUTMinna, and through the Schneider Battery Challenge organised by Schneider Electric and Enactus Nigeria, I helped lead an outreach programme for secondary school students. Our mission was to educate young minds on the critical role of batteries in our daily lives: their uses, advantages and environmental impact. As the world shifts towards sustainable energy solutions, it is crucial to raise awareness about battery technologies, their role in renewable energy storage, and the importance of eco-friendly disposal and recycling practices.

Why batteries matter for sustainability

Batteries power our lives, from mobile devices and electric vehicles to solar energy storage systems. However, improper disposal of batteries poses serious environmental threats, including soil and water contamination from hazardous chemicals such as lead, cadmium and lithium. By educating the younger generation, we are fostering a culture of responsible energy consumption and sustainable waste management, directly aligned with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals, particularly:

  • SDG 7 (Affordable & Clean Energy) — promoting efficient and sustainable battery storage solutions to support renewable energy.
  • SDG 12 (Responsible Consumption & Production) — encouraging proper battery disposal, recycling and eco-friendly innovations.
  • SDG 13 (Climate Action) — reducing electronic waste and advocating for green energy transitions.

Beyond the knowledge-sharing, this outreach was an opportunity to network with fellow Enactus members, exchange ideas and inspire students to become sustainability advocates. Seeing their enthusiasm and curiosity reaffirmed my belief in the power of education and community-driven change.

Sustainability starts with awareness, and awareness leads to action.
